Photo Guide

The right photo changes everything

The photo you send sets the mood and the tone of the whole portrait. A good one lets your pet look their best while exploring their cosmic adventure, so it is worth taking a minute to get it right.

✓ What will work
✕ What will not work

The checklist

Six things that make a perfect photo

  • Taken at your pet’s eye level, not from above or below.
  • Well lit. Protip: sit your pet in front of a window, and use a chair if you need to.
  • A close-up portrait with all their features in frame. Not too far away.
  • Sharp and in focus. Check that you can see the details clearly.
  • The original photo, not a screenshot of another photo.
  • A headshot. A full body shot is not necessary.

Things to avoid

And six things that will not

  • Taken from far away, so it is not really a headshot.
  • Their face is obstructed by a hand, a toy or a blanket.
  • Too dark to make out their features.
  • Blurry, or low resolution.
  • The wrong angle, either from above or below.
  • Eyes closed, or an unflattering close-up.

Not sure if your photo is good enough?

Send it over anyway. I will tell you honestly whether it will work, and if it will not, I will tell you exactly what to try instead. There is no charge and no obligation for that.

If you are photographing a pet who has passed, do not worry about any of the rules above. Send me whatever you have and I will do everything I can with it.
